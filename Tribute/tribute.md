# **Introducing the photographers who have had the greatest influence on my work**

## **My most impactful teachers from Warsaw School of Fotography (WSFoto)**

* Marian Schmidt  
  Marian Schmidt (1945–2018) was a Polish artist, photographer, mathematician, and the co-founder and director of the Warsaw School of Photography and Graphic Design. Born in Żyrardów, Poland, his family emigrated shortly after World War II, and he spent his childhood in Caracas, Venezuela, where he first began photographing at the age of twelve. Although he initially studied engineering and obtained a Ph.D. in mathematics from Brandeis University in the United States, his passion for visual arts eventually led him to a full-time career in photography and film.  
  Throughout his career, Schmidt worked as an assistant producer in New York, shot documentaries in Saudi Arabia, and joined the prestigious Rapho photo agency in Paris, aligning with the traditions of humanist reportage photography. Later in his life, he transitioned toward a more abstract style of still-life imagery that he termed "photopainting." In Poland, he dedicated much of his time to education, teaching at the Academy of Fine Arts in Poznań and ZPAF before founding WSFoto in 2000, where he taught expressive photography and the phenomenology of the image.  
  [Marian Schmidt Foundation Biography](https://marianschmidt.org/biography/)  
* Tadeusz Rolke  
  Tadeusz Rolke (born 1929\) is widely regarded as a classic master of Polish photojournalism. Born in Warsaw, he was a member of the Grey Ranks during the occupation and survived the Warsaw Uprising as a liaison. His early interest in photography was fueled by wartime illustrated magazines, and he took his first surviving photographs in 1944\. Despite his studies being interrupted by political imprisonment during the Stalinist era in 1951, Rolke returned to photography upon his release, working in photo-technical laboratories before becoming a staff photographer for magazines such as *Stolica*, *Polska*, and *Świat*.  
  Rolke spent the 1970s living in Germany, collaborating with prominent international publications like *Stern*, *Die Zeit*, and *Der Spiegel*, creating renowned series such as "Fischmarkt." Drawn back to Poland by the Solidarity movement in the 1980s, he extensively documented the country's social and political transformations, capturing everything from the ruins of postwar Warsaw to the vibrant, shifting realities of the 1989 democratic transition.  
  [Tadeusz Rolke on Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/artist/tadeusz-rolke)  
* Lorne Liesenfeld  
  Lorne Liesenfeld (born 1960\) is a photographer, educator, and author closely associated with the Warsaw School of Photography and Graphic Design. Born in Hamilton, Ontario, Canada, to German immigrant parents, Liesenfeld's background spans multiple cultures. He is well-known in the Polish photography community as an active lecturer and workshop facilitator.  
  He is the author of the 2015 publication *"Cicha obecność fotografii"* (The Silent Presence of Photography) and has been a frequent collaborator on major photographic exhibitions and educational programs, including the Fotofestiwal in Łódź. His teachings often focus on the subtle, expressive qualities of the photographic medium.  
  [Lorne Liesenfeld on Fototapeta](https://fototapeta.art.pl/2023/lrn.php)  
* Stanisław Woś  
  Stanisław Woś (1951–2011) was a distinguished Polish photographer, painter, draughtsman, and a prominent lecturer at both the Warsaw School of Photography and the Nikon Academy. Born in Jeżowe, he graduated from the State College of Fine Arts (PWSSP) in Gdańsk. Woś was a monumental figure in the Polish photographic landscape of the 1990s, notably founding and leading the "PAcamera" club in Suwałki, which became one of the most vital photographic galleries and artistic centers in the country.  
  Recognized as a master of landscape photography, Woś’s work frequently ventured into the mystical and the transcendental. He pushed technical boundaries by exposing multiple negatives to create highly symbolic, unrepeatable, expressive images that dealt with themes of fragility, time, and the underlying beauty of nature. His work remains in the collections of major institutions like the Museum of Art in Łódź and the Centre for Contemporary Art in Warsaw.  
  [Stanisław Woś on Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/pl/tworca/stanislaw-j-wos)

## **Photographers who have inspired me**

* Annie Leibovitz  
  American portrait photographer renowned for her intimate, dramatic portraits of celebrities, often featured in Rolling Stone and Vanity Fair.  
  [Annie Leibovitz on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Leibovitz)  
* Abbas Attar  
  Iranian photographer and Magnum Photos member known for his photojournalism covering conflicts and major essays on world religions.  
  [Abbas Attar on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_(photographer))  
* Alfred Stieglitz  
  Key figure in America's acceptance of photography as a serious art form, promoting both Pictorialism and "straight" modern photography.  
  [Alfred Stieglitz at MFA](https://www.mfa.org/exhibitions/alfred-stieglitz-and-early-modern-photography)  
* Anne Brigman  
  American amateur photographer and one of the original members of the Photo-Secession movement, known for her naturalistic nudes in primal landscapes.  
  [Anne Brigman on WikiArt](https://www.wikiart.org/en/anne-brigman)  
* Ansel Adams  
  Iconic American landscape photographer and environmentalist famed for his striking black-and-white images of the American West.  
  [Ansel Adams at Center for Creative Photography](https://ccp.arizona.edu/artists/ansel-adams/)  
* Arnold Newman  
  American photographer who pioneered the environmental portrait by placing subjects in settings representative of their professions.  
  [Arnold Newman at Howard Greenberg Gallery](https://www.howardgreenberg.com/artists/88-arnold-newman/)  
* Brassaï  
  French-Hungarian photographer known for capturing the moody, romantic, and working-class character of Paris at night.  
  [Brassaï at National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artists/13567-brassai)  
* Christopher Anderson  
  Magnum photographer recognized for his emotionally charged documentary work and striking portraits, including his images of Haitian refugees.  
  [Christopher Anderson at World Press Photo](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/christopher-anderson)  
* Cornell Capa  
  A passionate "concerned photographer" who used his camera to educate and advocate for social change, and the founding director of the ICP.  
  [Cornell Capa at ICP](https://www.icp.org/exhibitions/cornell-capa-concerned-photographer)  
* Don McCullin  
  British photojournalist highly regarded for his stark war photography and poignant images of the unemployed and impoverished.  
  [Don McCullin on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_McCullin)  
* Dorothea Lange  
  American documentary photographer famed for her Depression-era work with the Farm Security Administration, which humanized the era's struggles.  
  [Dorothea Lange on MoMA Youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21nPEFybmtQ)  
* Edward Hartwig  
  Celebrated Polish photographer who blended pictorialist traditions with modernism, creating stylized and poetic landscapes and portraits.  
  [Edward Hartwig artwork at Muzeum Warszawy](https://kolekcje.muzeumwarszawy.pl/pl/teksty/miasto-od-a-do-z/edward-hartwig-fotograf-xx-wieku)  
* Krzysztof Gierałtowski  
  Insightful Polish portraitist known for capturing the psychological depth and individual character of prominent figures in Polish culture.  
  [Krzysztof Gierałtowski on Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/artist/krzysztof-gieraltowski)  
* Harry Callahan  
  American photographer and educator celebrated for his formal innovations and deeply personal images of his wife Eleanor and everyday street scenes.  
  [Harry Callahan on HASTA](http://www.hasta-standrews.com/birthdays/2025/10/22/harry-callahan-1922-1999)  
* Henri Cartier-Bresson  
  French humanist photographer and early adopter of 35mm film, universally considered the master of candid photography and "the decisive moment."  
  [Henri Cartier-Bresson on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Cartier-Bresson)  
* Jan Saudek  
  Leading Czech photographer celebrated for his surreal, hand-colored depictions of figures arranged in dreamlike, theatrical spaces.  
  [Jan Saudek on Artnet](https://www.artnet.com/artists/jan-saudek/13)  
* Karl Lagerfeld  
  German fashion designer, creative director, and photographer who shaped modern high fashion and produced notable fashion photography.  
  [Karl Lagerfeld on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Lagerfeld)  
* Robert Mapplethorpe  
  American photographer recognized for his large-scale, highly stylized black-and-white portraits, floral still lifes, and controversial nudes.  
  [Robert Mapplethorpe on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mapplethorpe)  
* Marek Karewicz  
  Polish photographer closely associated with the jazz and rock 'n' roll scenes, renowned for his album covers and concert photography.  
  [Marek Karewicz on Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/article/how-rock-n-roll-conquered-communist-censorship)  
* Minor White  
  American photographer and educator whose work merged intense observation of nature with deep spiritual and philosophical meaning.  
  [Minor White on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_White)  
* Paolo Roversi  
  Contemporary Italian fashion photographer famous for his romantic, softly lit, and ethereal portraits, often shot on large-format Polaroid film.  
  [Paolo Roversi on Artnet](https://www.artnet.com/artists/paolo-roversi/)  
* Peter Lindbergh  
  German fashion photographer known for his cinematic, starkly realistic black-and-white images that redefined beauty standards in the 1990s.  
  [Peter Lindbergh at Gagosian](https://gagosian.com/artists/peter-lindbergh/)  
* Richard Avedon  
  Influential American fashion and portrait photographer whose minimalist aesthetic and stark white backgrounds captured the essence of his subjects.  
  [Richard Avedon on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Avedon)  
* Robert Capa  
  Legendary Hungarian-American war photographer who co-founded Magnum Photos and bravely covered major conflicts with an immersive, up-close style.  
  [Robert Capa at Musée de la Libération](https://www.museeliberation-leclerc-moulin.paris.fr/en/expositions/robert-capa-war-photographer)  
* Robert Doisneau  
  French humanist photographer famed for his playful, poetic, and often surreal images capturing the everyday life of Paris streets.  
  [Robert Doisneau on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Doisneau)  
* Rudolf Koppitz  
  Austrian photographer whose work bridged Pictorialism and modernism, best known for his meticulously crafted and graceful motion studies.  
  [Rudolf Koppitz on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Koppitz)
